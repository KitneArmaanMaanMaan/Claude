01 Recommendation

Squid is building the vendor-neutral system of record for electricity network models — and nobody has priced the company yet.

Every network operator's model of its own grid is scattered across DIgSILENT PowerFactory, Siemens PSS®E, IPSA, CYME, ETAP, GIS systems and spreadsheets, in incompatible formats with no shared version history. Squid imports all of them and resolves them into one governed, versioned model with lineage, topology validation and scenario management. Planners query it in a browser; AI agents run power-system analysis against it. Their own line: “the grid has a repo now.”

Nine months after incorporation, with three people, they are in production at National Grid Electricity Distribution, powering the FlexPortal that NGED launched publicly in February 2026. And per the UK register they have never priced a round — share capital stands at £1 since incorporation, no share allotment has ever been filed, and both founders remain persons with significant control at 25–50% each.

A lead investor here would be setting the first price on a company that is already inside a Tier-1 utility. That is a rare shape, and it will not stay open.

02 Why this company

  1. A

    The founders ran the function they are selling into.

    Conor Jones spent nine years at National Grid, rising from power systems engineer to Digital Product Line Director — reported as the youngest Director at National Grid Transmission, with 60+ engineers and a £10M+ annual budget — then went to Octopus Energy as Head of Product. George Kolokotronis was Head of Tech at Octopus Energy, via AWS and Cambridge, and has been a company director since he was nineteen. This is not ex-FAANG engineers discovering energy. They are the customer.

  2. B

    They took the one position an incumbent structurally cannot.

    Siemens, DIgSILENT, Eaton and ETAP all sell seats of their own simulation engines. The entire value of Squid is neutrality across all five formats. An incumbent shipping this would be building the thing that makes its own file format irrelevant. That is not a moat a feature release erodes — and it is why the category has stayed unclaimed while the top five vendors between them hold just under half of a multi-billion-dollar market.

  3. C

    They got a Tier-1 utility into production at three people.

    National Grid does not buy software from three-person companies. Squid cleared ISO 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type I and II first, then entered through a stakeholder-facing wedge — making network data legible to third parties — rather than a core-systems replacement. That is a deliberate go-to-market, and it is the hardest thing on this page to fake.

There is a fourth unlock specific to this moment: AI cannot be applied to grid planning until there is a clean, governed model to apply it to. This is exactly the argument Creandum made for Rillet — that NetSuite's data syntax had been “muddled by countless customizations,” so the AI opportunity in finance required rebuilding the general ledger first.

Network models are in that condition, for that reason. Squid is rebuilding the ledger.

The wedge alone does not make a fund-returner, and the memo should say so. A 25% share of £250M is ~£60M ARR — a €600M–€1B company. The case for more rests entirely on expansion, and it is a specific claim rather than a hand-wave: whoever owns the approved network model owns the workflows that depend on it — connection-queue management, reinforcement planning, flexibility procurement, outage planning, asset investment.

Those budgets sit inside a power-system software market variously estimated at $6.75B growing ~8% or $12.1B growing ~13%. The spread is wide enough that I treat it as several billion, compounding rather than as a number.

08 The bear case

Argued as hard as I can

  1. Short, slow buyer list. ~40 TSOs and ~190 large DSOs in Europe, buying on 12–24 month cycles gated by regulated price-control allowances. Squid could be excellent and still grow at the speed of RIIO.
  2. The wedge might be a feature. Version control for network models could be absorbed into a PowerFactory release, or delivered as a consultancy engagement.
  3. Expansion is assumed, not proven. No evidence yet that they can sell a second product.
  4. Team of three against an enterprise motion and five proprietary file formats.
  5. A US entity exists (Squid Group, Inc.) alongside Squid Labs Ltd. The register shows no corporate PSC over the UK company, so no flip has been recorded — but Delaware is the YC default and this should be settled before investing.
  6. Name collision with an unrelated crypto company and a well-known proxy server. At least one commercial database has already merged their funding histories.

What would need to be true: NGED is a paid production contract rather than an innovation-budget pilot; at least one more operator has signed, ideally outside the UK, proving the model is not a Britain-specific regulatory artefact; the founders can name the second and third products with a design partner for one; utility ACVs can reach £300k+ within 24 months.

The single fact that would most change this recommendation is whether anyone is paying, and how much. Everything public shows deployment and a marquee logo; nothing public shows revenue. That is the first question on the call, and it cannot be answered from desk research.

09 Why Creandum

White space. Across all 154 companies in Creandum's portfolio, climate and energy is the coldest active sector — six active companies, nothing new since terralayr in October 2024. Grid software is not represented at all, and there is no conflict: terralayr trades energy flexibility, Enode sits on devices, Monta on charging. None touch network planning for operators.

Shape match. A vendor-neutral software layer above incumbent tools, days-not-months time-to-value, a marquee industrial logo, real-world data compounding — that is the Sereact pattern, in Creandum's own words:

Sereact's approach is to build a foundational model that works across all relevant robotics platforms… this real world deployment of their models provides valuable data for training.Creandum, Backing Sereact, January 2025

Non-consensus by construction. Over 90% of the Euro Seed 50 is AI-native and the vertical-agent lane is saturated. Squid is AI-native and in a sector nobody is crowding. It is the closest thing on the longlist to “before it's obvious.”

Not on the Euro Seed 50. That list ran in June 2025; Squid incorporated in December. Creandum converted 8 of those 50 into investments, which makes the list their funnel rather than their publicity — and means this company has not been through it.

Limitations of this memo

No primary research with people. Founder, customer and expert calls were out of scope for this pass, so everything here is desk-based — and the single most important open question, contracted ARR, is precisely the one a twenty-minute founder call would answer.

Market sizing is constructed. The bottom-up table uses public buyer counts with my own ACV assumptions, stated rather than buried.

Sourcing breadth. Company registries were used to verify finalists rather than as bulk discovery; EIC, Vinnova and Innovate UK grant sweeps and patent filings were not run.

Every factual claim in this memo is traced to a URL with an access date in the accompanying source appendix. Five items are explicitly flagged as unverified rather than smoothed over.
