# Investment Memo — Squid
### Recommendation: lead the seed
**Squid Labs Ltd · London, UK · founded 22 December 2025 · squid.energy**
Prepared 11 September 2026. All claims sourced in `SOURCES.md`; unverified items flagged inline.

---

## 1. Recommendation in one paragraph

Squid is building the **model of record for electricity networks** — a vendor-neutral,
versioned repository that federates the network models currently scattered across
PowerFactory, PSS®E, IPSA, CYME, ETAP, GIS systems and spreadsheets, and makes them
queryable by planners and by AI agents. Nine months after incorporation, with three people,
they are in production at **National Grid Electricity Distribution**, powering the FlexPortal
that NGED launched publicly in February 2026. Per the UK register, they have **not priced a
single round** — share capital stands at £1 since incorporation, both founders still hold
25–50% each, and no share allotment has ever been filed. Creandum can lead the first priced
round, set the terms, and take a full seed position in a category where no incumbent can
structurally compete. I recommend leading a **$12–15M seed**.

---

## 2. Why *this* company — not the sector

Three things separate Squid from every other energy-software company on the longlist.

**The founders ran the function they are selling into.** Conor Jones spent nine years at
National Grid, rising from power systems engineer to Digital Product Line Director — reported
as the youngest Director at National Grid Transmission, with 60+ engineers and a £10M+ annual
budget — then went to Octopus Energy as Head of Product. George Kolokotronis was Head of Tech
at Octopus Energy, came via AWS and Cambridge, and has been a company director since he was
19. This is not "ex-FAANG engineers discover energy." They are the customer.

**They chose the one position an incumbent cannot take.** Siemens, DIgSILENT, Eaton and ETAP
all sell seats of their own simulation engines. The entire value of Squid is being neutral
across all five formats. An incumbent shipping this would be building the thing that makes
their own file format irrelevant. That is not a moat that erodes with a feature release; it
is a structural conflict, and it is why this category has stayed unclaimed while the top five
vendors between them hold just under half of a multi-billion-dollar market.

**They got a Tier-1 utility into production at three people.** National Grid does not buy
software from three-person companies. Squid got in by clearing ISO 27001:2022 and SOC 2 Type
I and II first, and by entering through a stakeholder-facing wedge — making network data
legible to third parties — rather than through a core-systems replacement. That is a
deliberate, sophisticated go-to-market, and it is the single hardest thing to fake.

## 3. Why *now*

Three unlocks, all dated, all outside the company's control.

1. **The connection queue became the binding constraint on European electrification.**
   ~1,700 GW of projects are waiting for grid connection across Europe. €100B of renewables
   and storage sits stuck in *distribution* queues across eight countries — 375 GW of
   renewables, 455 GW of storage. UK waits run to 10–15 years. Planning throughput, not
   generation capacity, is now what stands between Europe and its targets.

2. **Regulation turned data quality into a licence condition.** Ofgem's Data Best Practice
   guidance sits under **RIIO-ED2 Special Condition 9.5 (Digitalisation)**. DNOs must publish
   Digitalisation Strategy and Action Plans and make network data understandable and usable
   by external stakeholders. Squid is not selling a productivity improvement; it is selling
   into a regulatory obligation with a deadline. That is why the NGED deal happened at all.

3. **The money is arriving.** €584B of EU grid investment is required 2020–2030, roughly
   €170B of it digitalisation. EU electricity demand is forecast up ~60% by 2030.

There is a fourth unlock that is specific to this moment: **AI cannot be applied to grid
planning until there is a clean, governed model to apply it to.** This is precisely the
argument Creandum made for Rillet — that NetSuite's data syntax had been "muddled by
countless customizations," so the AI opportunity in finance required rebuilding the general
ledger first. Network models are in exactly that condition, for exactly that reason. Squid is
rebuilding the ledger.

## 6. The cap table — and why this is the moment

From Companies House, company 16925657, read 11 September 2026:

- Incorporated 22 Dec 2025 with a **statement of capital of GBP 1**.
- Complete filing history: the incorporation, and three registered-office changes. **Nothing else.**
- **No SH01. No capital increase. No amended articles. No corporate PSC.**
- Both founders remain PSCs at **25–50% of shares and votes each**.

A priced round essentially always produces an SH01 and amended articles. Their absence means
the YC, 20VC, Leap Forward and 33East money is on unconverted SAFEs or ASAs, and **no
investor has yet set a price on this company**. For a fund that leads 60–70% of its seeds and
needs real ownership, that is as clean an entry as this exercise is likely to produce.

*Caveat: allotments must be filed within a month, so a round closed in the last few weeks
could still be in transit. Re-check the register before any term sheet.*

## 7. Fund math

A €500M fund needs outcomes that move it, so the arithmetic has to be stated rather than implied.

- Entry: lead a **$12–15M seed on a ~$60M post** → **~20–22% ownership**, priced first.
- Dilution through Series A–D at ~20% per round → **~8–9% at exit**.
- At a **€1B** outcome: ~€85M back = **0.17x** the fund. Respectable, not decisive.
- At a **€2B** outcome: ~€170M back = **0.34x** the fund. Fund-shaping.
- At **€3B+**: **0.5x+** from a single seed position.

So the investment is a bet on the expansion case specifically, not on the wedge. The wedge
buys the position; the workflow layer is what makes it matter. That is the honest framing,
and it is the same shape as the Rillet bet.

## 8. Risks, and what would need to be true

**The bear case, argued properly:**

1. **Short, slow buyer list.** ~40 TSOs and ~190 large DSOs in Europe, buying on 12–24 month
   cycles gated by regulated price-control allowances. Squid could be excellent and still
   grow at the speed of RIIO.
2. **The wedge might be a feature.** Version control for network models could be absorbed
   into a PowerFactory release or delivered as a consultancy engagement.
3. **Expansion is assumed, not proven.** No evidence yet that they can sell a second product.
4. **Team of three** against an enterprise motion and five proprietary file formats.
5. **A US entity exists** (Squid Group, Inc.) alongside Squid Labs Ltd. The register shows no
   corporate PSC over the UK company, so no flip has been recorded — but Delaware is the YC
   default and this should be settled before investing.
6. **Name collision** with an unrelated crypto company and a well-known proxy server; at
   least one data provider has already conflated their funding histories.

**What would need to be true for this to work:**
- NGED is a paid production contract, not an innovation-budget pilot.
- At least one more network operator has signed — ideally outside the UK, proving the model
  is not a Britain-specific regulatory artefact.
- The founders can articulate the second and third products, with a design partner for one.
- Utility ACVs can reach £300k+ within 24 months.

**The single fact that would most change this recommendation:** whether anyone is paying,
and how much. Everything public shows deployment and a marquee logo; nothing public shows
revenue. That is the first question on the call, and it cannot be answered from desk research.

## 9. Why *Creandum*

- **White space.** Climate and energy is Creandum's coldest active sector — six active
  companies, nothing new since terralayr in October 2024. Grid software is not represented at
  all, and there is no conflict: terralayr trades energy flexibility, Enode and Monta sit on
  devices and charging. None of them touch network planning for operators.
- **Shape match.** Vendor-neutral software layer above incumbent hardware and tools, days-not-
  months time-to-value, marquee industrial logo, real-world data compounding — that is the
  Sereact pattern, described in Creandum's own words. The Rillet argument about rebuilding
  the ledger before AI can work applies almost line for line.
- **Non-consensus by construction.** Over 90% of the Euro Seed 50 is AI-native and the
  vertical-agent lane is saturated. Squid is AI-native *and* in a sector nobody is crowding.
  It is the closest thing on the longlist to "before it's obvious."
- **Not on the Euro Seed 50.** The list ran in June 2025; Squid incorporated in December.
  Creandum converted 8 of those 50 into investments, so the list is their funnel, not their
  publicity. This company has not been through it.
- **Ownership is available and unpriced.** Rare, and it will not last.
