# What Creandum actually likes

Not "what have they bought recently" — that produces a list of AI agent companies and a
consensus answer. The right question is **what has actually made them money**, because that
is the shape a partner recognises before they can explain why.

## The outcome cohort

From their own CMS: 47 past/exited positions and 52 active positions entered before 2022.
The verified large outcomes:

|---|---|---|
| Spotify | 2008 | Music streaming |

Plus Neo4j, Epidemic Sound, Vivino, Pleo, Factorial, Tide, Taxfix, Billie, swan, Voi, Gopuff.

**This is not a deep-tech fund's record.** It is a consumer, marketplace and fintech record,
with AI and frontier hardware added only in the last two years. Of the twelve largest
outcomes, **eleven changed the behaviour of ordinary people or small businesses.** Not one was
a laboratory.

## The pattern, stated precisely

Every one of those companies entered a market that informed people believed was
**structurally closed**, and opened it by collapsing a cost or an access barrier by roughly
an order of magnitude.

| Company | What everyone believed | What collapsed |
|---|---|---|
| Spotify | "Labels will never license; piracy is free" | Access to all recorded music: €15 an album → €10 a month |
| iZettle | "Card acceptance below £X volume is uneconomic; banks have run the numbers" | Terminal cost: ~€1,000 + merchant account → a €20 dongle |
| Klarna | "Unsecured online credit at checkout is a fraud disaster" | Underwriting cost per transaction → near zero |
| Depop | "Secondhand clothing isn't a real market" | Cost of selling one garment → a photo and a tap |
| Trade Republic | "You cannot run a brokerage at that price" | Trade cost: ~€10 → €1 |
| Kry | "Regulators and doctors will never accept remote consultation" | Access to a GP: days and a journey → minutes on a phone |
| Epidemic Sound | "Music rights for video are impossible to clear" | Licensing a track: a negotiation → a subscription |
| Lovable | "People who can't code can't build software" | Building an app: €50k and an agency → a prompt |

**The engine is always the same: something that cost a lot or was available to few becomes
cheap or available to many, and a new behaviour appears that did not exist before.** The
technology is the means. The behaviour change is the company.

## What this implies for the pick

1. **A market widely believed to be closed** is the contrarian criterion, expressed in
   Creandum's own language. "Everyone is almost against it" and "everyone believes this
   market cannot be served" are the same sentence from different directions.
2. **A specific mechanism that collapses cost or access by ~10x.** Not "better", not "AI-
   powered" — an order of magnitude on price, time, or eligibility. If you cannot name the
   multiple, there is no thesis.
3. **A new behaviour at scale**, ideally consumer or small-business. This is where Creandum's
   pattern recognition lives and where their €1B+ outcomes have come from.
4. **Category creation, not category competition.** None of the winners were a better version
   of an existing product.

## Where this leaves the previous two picks

- **Squid** — no cost collapse, no behaviour change, no closed market. A better tool for
  ~230 buyers who already buy tools. Off-pattern.
- **Frontier Computing** — genuinely heretical, but it is a laboratory, and Creandum has
  never made money on a laboratory. The mechanism is a scientific claim rather than a cost
  collapse, and there is no behaviour to change until the science lands. It belongs on a
  frontier fund's list, not at the top of Creandum's.

Both were answers to questions I had framed myself. This pass sources against the pattern
above instead.

## What the pattern says to look for in 2026

Three places where a closed market is being forced open *right now*, in Europe specifically:

- **Regulation prying markets open.** The EU has legislated more market-opening in 2025–27
  than in the previous decade: the Data Act (connected-device data must be released to
  owners), eIDAS 2.0 and the EU Digital Identity Wallet, the Instant Payments Regulation,
  FiDA, the Right to Repair Directive, and Digital Product Passports. Each of these takes a
  market an incumbent had locked and makes it legally open. This is a European edge a US fund
  structurally cannot see.
- **Cost collapse from AI applied to a supply constraint** — not to a workflow. Where the
  binding constraint was *the number of qualified humans*, not their speed.
- **A physical or scientific unlock** that changes a unit cost by an order of magnitude.
