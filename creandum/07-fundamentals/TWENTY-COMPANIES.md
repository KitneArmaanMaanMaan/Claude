# Twenty companies, analysed on fundamentals

Scored against the pattern in `WHAT-CREANDUM-ACTUALLY-LIKES.md`, not against founder CVs or
cap tables. The four questions asked of each:

1. **What closed market does this company's existence claim is open?**
2. **What is the specific collapse** — the multiple on cost, time, or eligibility?
3. **What new behaviour appears** if it works, and at what scale?
4. **What kills it?**

Stage is a gate, not a score. Where a company may have moved past seed, that is flagged.

---

## Tier 1 — the pattern in full

### 1. Poppins · France · P2P rental of everyday objects
**Closed market:** peer-to-peer object rental is a startup graveyard. Peerby, Streetbank,
Rentoid, Sharery — all died on the same rock: not enough liquidity in a neighbourhood, too
much friction per transaction, and nobody trusts a stranger with their drill. Investors have
seen this pitch and passed on it for fifteen years.
**The collapse:** an electric drill is used about 12 minutes across its entire life and costs
€80–200. Renting one costs €2–4. That is a 20–50x collapse in the cost of *access to a
capability* — and it applies to camping gear, party equipment, baby kit, carpet cleaners,
raclette machines. The category is enormous and almost entirely dormant capital sitting in
cupboards.
**Why now:** the thing that killed the predecessors was liquidity, and the unlock is not
technology — it is a founding team that has built neighbourhood-density consumer liquidity
once before. Too Good To Go solved exactly this problem shape (hyperlocal, low-value,
high-frequency, trust-dependent) and scaled it past 100M users.
**Evidence:** launched April 2025; **350,000+ users** reported by mid-2026; 20,000+ items
listed early; Belgium launch 2026, UK planned.
**Team:** Lucie Basch (co-founder, Too Good To Go) with Jonas Mallisse (launched TGTG Belgium,
head of international expansion), Franco Prontera (CEO TGTG Belgium), Loïck Le Digabel (CTO,
ex-BlaBlaCar, ex-Back Market). *Noted, not scored — but this is the single best
consumer-marketplace founding team available in Europe at this stage.*
**Stage:** angels only (incl. Frédéric Mazzella of BlaBlaCar, Alexandre Mars) plus Bpifrance,
LCL and Crédit Coopératif **loans** — i.e. non-dilutive. No institutional priced round found.
**What kills it:** monetisation. A €3 rental cannot carry a take rate that funds a company;
TGTG had the same problem and solved it with volume plus merchant fees. If Poppins cannot
find the equivalent — insurance, local-business supply, subscription — it is a beloved app
with no P&L. Second risk: logistics. Handover friction is the thing that actually kills these.
**Creandum fit:** near-perfect. Consumer marketplace, France (they have Paris deals), no
conflict, category creation, behaviour change. This is Depop-and-Spotify-shaped.
⚠️ *Disambiguation: there is an unrelated French "Poppins" in dyslexia neurotech with an €8M
Series A led by Eurazeo. Different company. At least one database conflates them.*

### 2. Revyze · France · social learning
**Closed market:** "teenagers will not study voluntarily", and "edtech consumer subscriptions
don't work". Every consumer edtech of the last decade either became a B2B school sale or died.
**The collapse:** the cost of producing a revision resource falls from a publisher's textbook
cycle to a 45-second phone video made by a 16-year-old who sat the exam last year. Supply goes
from a few thousand professional authors to every student in the country.
**Why now:** short-form video is the native literacy of the cohort, and the peer is more
credible than the publisher.
**Evidence:** the strongest consumer traction in this entire set — **1M+ users, and over one
third of all French 9th graders** used it to prepare for the *brevet*. Hit #1 on the French
App Store. Founded 2022 by Guillaume Perrot (ex-Alan), Anatole Blanc (ex-Auto1), Florent
Sciberras (ex-Airbnb).
**Stage:** ⚠️ **$6M seed led by Speedinvest and Moonfire, October 2024.** Two years old. A
Series A may well have happened since — **verify before pitching.** If it has, it is out.
**What kills it:** national-curriculum lock-in. One third of French 9th graders is a
phenomenal number and also a warning — the content is curriculum-specific, so each new country
is a cold start. Monetisation of teenagers is brutal.
**Creandum fit:** very high if still at seed. Consumer, category-creating, Kahoot!-adjacent
(which they know how to underwrite).

### 3. Spielwerk · Germany · playable games as social content
**Closed market:** "ordinary people cannot make games." Game creation has always required a
studio, an engine, and years. UGC gaming has exactly one winner (Roblox) and a long list of
corpses.
**The collapse:** describe an idea in a sentence, get a playable game. Cost of producing a
mini-game falls from months and a team to a prompt. This is the Lovable insight applied to
interactive content rather than software.
**Why now:** models only crossed the "generates a *playable* game, not a broken one"
threshold in the last year.
**Evidence:** thin. A working app, a TikTok-style feed of user-made games, remixing, high-score
leaderboards. Created by Eike Drescher.
**Stage:** appears pre-seed / unfunded. A US analogue, Remix, raised $6.75M seed (Archetype,
Coinbase Ventures, Variant) — so the category is being priced, and Europe has not been.
**What kills it:** retention. AI-generated mini-games are trivially easy to make and therefore
trivially disposable; the feed may have no reason to exist after week two. Roblox works
because of social graph and economy, not because of content volume.
**Creandum fit:** high on shape, low on evidence. This is the one where a partner's judgement
about *whether the behaviour sticks* is the entire investment. Exactly the call Creandum made
on Lovable.

### 4. Wenup · UK · prenuptial and cohabitation agreements
**Closed market:** "ordinary people cannot afford a lawyer, and legal work cannot be
unbundled." Family law is a closed shop priced for the wealthy.
**The collapse:** a prenup from a London firm costs £2,000–5,000 per person and takes weeks.
Wenup is **£690 each, including the independent legal advice both parties are legally
required to receive** for the agreement to hold. Roughly 4–7x on price, and a collaborative
flow instead of two adversarial lawyers.
**Why now:** cohabitation is the fastest-growing family form in Europe and has almost no legal
protection; AI makes the drafting marginal-cost-zero while the regulated advice step stays
human, which is what makes it enforceable rather than a novelty template.
**Evidence:** live product, transparent pricing, named founders (Mohsin Zaidi; James Brookner,
who built and scaled Deazy and spent a decade as a VC).
**Stage:** no institutional round found.
**What kills it:** it is a one-time purchase in a life event with no repeat, so CAC has to be
paid back on a single £1,380 transaction. And it keeps a human lawyer in the loop, which caps
gross margin. Fund-returner shape is the real question, not whether it works.
**Creandum fit:** good on pattern, questionable on size. Would need to become "the legal layer
for life events" — prenup, cohabitation, wills, divorce, property — to be a €1B company.

### 5. Frontier Computing · UK · biological neurons as compute
**Closed market:** compute is fabricated in a foundry. Nobody argues about the substrate.
**The collapse:** not yet a cost collapse — a *substrate* claim. The brain runs ~86B neurons
on ~20W; training GPUs draw 700–2,700W each. If the substrate works, the energy multiple is
enormous.
**Why now:** the ceiling that stalled wetware at ~1M neurons is a diffusion limit (necrotic
cores past 300–400µm), and 2025–26 protocols now grow cortical organoids past 3mm without one.
**Evidence:** a culture trained to play Frogger — 92% peak cross rate after one hour of live
learning. A dated, falsifiable roadmap: 100M neurons by end-2026, 500M by January 2027.
**Stage:** $10M pre-seed on SAFEs, led by General Catalyst.
**What kills it:** neurons die in ~6 months; the clock gap to silicon is ~9 orders of
magnitude; cultures are not reproducible and ML requires reproducibility; the vascularisation
claim is **unpublished and unreplicated**. P(zero) above 70%.
**Creandum fit:** the honest answer is *off-pattern*. Creandum has never made money on a
laboratory. This belongs in the portfolio as a one-in-forty barbell position, not as the
centrepiece. It remains the most genuinely revolutionary company in the set.

## Tier 2 — real insight, a specific doubt

### 6. KugelAudio · Germany · on-prem multilingual speech
**Closed market:** "enterprise voice AI must run in someone else's cloud." For European
health, public sector and finance that has meant simply not deploying it.
**The collapse:** eligibility, not price — it makes voice AI *legal* for buyers who could not
use it at all. 30+ languages and dialects natively (Bavarian German, Northern Irish English,
Yemeni Arabic) including the cases that break generic TTS: emails, phone numbers, product names.
**Evidence:** open-sourced the first model, **150k+ Hugging Face downloads** before YC.
**Kills it:** TTS quality is commoditising fast; the defensible company may be the deployment
and compliance wrapper, which is smaller. Adjacent to Creandum's GetVocal (Nov 2025).

### 7. Mozart AI · UK · AI co-production DAW
**Closed market:** "you need a studio and an engineer to make a record."
**The collapse:** production cost per track from thousands to near zero, *without* taking
creative control away — deliberately positioned against Suno-style one-click generation.
**Evidence:** **100k+ users in beta; 10M+ Spotify streams in two months.** Founder Sundar
Arvind was signed to Warner's Spinnin' Records at 14.
**Stage:** ⚠️ **$6M seed led by Balderton, February 2026.** Seed is closed; eligible on the
task's wording but there is no round to lead.
**Kills it:** Ableton/Logic ship the same features; and the AI-music rights environment is
unresolved.

### 8. Squid · UK · model of record for electricity networks
**Closed market:** none, honestly. Its thesis — AI needs a clean, governed data layer — is
orthodox.
**The collapse:** none nameable. It is a better tool for ~230 buyers who already buy tools.
**Evidence:** the best in the set — in production at National Grid Electricity Distribution
nine months after incorporation, powering the FlexPortal launched February 2026. ISO 27001 and
SOC 2 already cleared.
**Stage:** no priced round.
**Verdict:** the highest-probability company here and the lowest-variance. Included because
it is genuinely good, and excluded from Tier 1 because it does not open a closed market.

### 9. Lamb Labs · UK · model weights hardcoded into silicon
**Closed market:** "weights live in memory and move to compute." The entire GPU industry is
memory-bandwidth engineering.
**The collapse:** claimed 20,000+ tok/s and 63× intelligence per watt versus a GPU.
**Kills it:** a chip that hardcodes a model cannot be updated, in a field where models change
monthly. Needs $100M+ before revenue. Team size listed as 0.

### 10. Tenet Industries · Sweden · mass-manufacturable strike drones
**Closed market:** "defence hardware is expensive by nature." Design-for-manufacture first,
then solve the software.
**The collapse:** a kamikaze quadcopter that assembles in two minutes with half the parts of
comparable systems.
**Kills it:** many European LPAs exclude lethal weapons — Creandum may be structurally unable.
Three people, founded 2026, YC money only.

### 11. Seeing Systems · UK · modular autonomous strike drones
**The evidence is the story:** **UK Royal Marine Commandos, other UK units and four NATO
forces** engaged, with prototypes shipping, at seven people. Claims >2x lifecycle cost
reduction through hardware modularity.
**Kills it:** same LPA problem; "European Anduril" is now a crowded trade, not a contrarian one.

### 12. GALVANY · Germany · heat pump platform
**Closed market:** "residential heat pump installation cannot be a venture business — it's a
fragmented trades market with no margin."
**The collapse:** end-to-end platform for installation, operation and optimisation, turning
dynamic electricity tariffs into measurable household savings.
**Evidence:** **profitable, with sevenfold revenue growth.** Rare at this stage.
**Stage:** ⚠️ €10M seed closed June 2026.
**Kills it:** it is an operationally heavy installer business wearing software clothes.

### 13. Ora Computing · Austria · model compression for edge deployment
**The collapse:** models small enough to run on energy-efficient edge hardware instead of
cloud GPUs. €3.5M seed, June 2026.
**Kills it:** classic feature-not-a-company risk; quantisation and distillation are being
commoditised in the open-source ecosystem monthly.

### 14. Vetnio · Sweden · veterinary admin
**Closed market:** "vet practices are too small and too poor to buy software."
**The collapse:** vets spend 40% of their time on admin, 20%+ on note-taking. Removing most of
that is effectively a 25% capacity increase in a profession with a severe shortage.
**Kills it:** small TAM; scribing is the most commoditised AI application there is.

### 15. Condor Energy · France · energy procurement for large consumers
**Closed market:** "only utilities and traders can optimise industrial energy procurement."
**The collapse:** brings trader-grade procurement to C&I buyers and data centres who
previously took whatever their supplier offered. Founders are ex-electricity traders and
energy physicists.
**Kills it:** thin wedge; incumbent brokers and suppliers defend this aggressively.

**My recommendation, if you want one: Poppins.** It is the only company in the set that is
simultaneously (a) attacking a market that informed people have watched kill startups for
fifteen years, (b) able to state its collapse as a hard multiple, (c) already showing
consumer-scale behaviour change, (d) shaped exactly like the companies that actually made
Creandum money, and (e) genuinely lead-able — the cap table is angels and state-bank debt.

**The best contrarian answer remains Frontier Computing** if the brief is strictly "the market
must be against it." **The safest is Squid.** They are three different questions.

  **This is the single most important gap in the set.**

## An honest gap in this pass

I built a thesis around **European regulatory unlocks** — the Data Act (connected-device data
must be released from 12 Sep 2026), eIDAS 2.0 and the EUDI Wallet (every member state must
offer one by end-2026), the Instant Payments Regulation, Right to Repair (national law from
31 July 2026), and Digital Product Passports (registry live 20 July 2026). Each takes a market
an incumbent had locked and opens it by law, and the Commission puts the Data Act alone at
**€273B of GDP by 2028**. It is the strongest European-specific edge I found, and a US fund
structurally cannot see it.

**I did not find a convincing seed-stage company attacking any of them.** That is either a
sourcing failure on my part or a genuine white space. Given these dates are weeks away, it is
worth a dedicated hunt — and it may be the most valuable thing in this document.

## One that got away, worth knowing

**OpenClaw.** Built by Austrian developer Peter Steinberger (founder of PSPDFKit), it went
from weekend project to **the most-starred repository on GitHub — 346k+ stars, past React and
the Linux kernel — in under five months.** It is now a US 501(c)(3) foundation sponsored by
OpenAI, NVIDIA, Microsoft and Tencent, and Steinberger has joined OpenAI. Europe produced the
most extraordinary distribution event in recent open source and captured none of it. Not
investable — but it is the sharpest possible illustration of why a European fund should be
sourcing from GitHub rather than from funding announcements.
