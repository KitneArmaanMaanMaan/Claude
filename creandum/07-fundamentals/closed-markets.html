What I could not verify, and one thing I could not find at all.

The regulatory unlock lane is empty — and that may be the most valuable line here

Europe has legislated more market-opening in 2025–27 than in the previous decade. Each of these takes a market an incumbent had locked and opens it by law:

  • EU Data Act — connected-device data must be released to owners and their chosen third parties. Applies to products placed on the market from 12 September 2026. The Commission puts it at €273B of GDP by 2028.
  • eIDAS 2.0 / EUDI Wallet — every member state must offer a digital identity wallet by end-2026, with selective disclosure built in.
  • Instant Payments Regulation — instant euro transfer as a mandatory standard; pay-by-bank already ~€850B and growing 30% a year, at 30–70% below card cost.
  • Right to Repair — national law from 31 July 2026. Digital Product Passports — registry live 20 July 2026.

I did not find a convincing seed-stage company attacking any of them. That is either a sourcing failure or a genuine white space. Given the dates are weeks away, it deserves a dedicated hunt.

One that got away

OpenClaw went from weekend project to the most-starred repository on GitHub in under five months — and Europe captured none of it.

Built by Austrian developer Peter Steinberger, founder of PSPDFKit. It passed React and the Linux kernel, reaching 346k+ stars. It is now a US 501(c)(3) foundation sponsored by OpenAI, NVIDIA, Microsoft and Tencent, and Steinberger has joined OpenAI. Not investable — but the sharpest possible argument for sourcing from GitHub rather than from funding announcements.
