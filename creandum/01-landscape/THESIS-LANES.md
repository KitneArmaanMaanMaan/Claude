# Phase 1 — Three thesis lanes
Chosen against the Phase 0 white-space map, not against generic VC taste.
Selection test for a lane: (a) Creandum has signalled appetite, (b) a specific
"why now" unlock exists, (c) public sourcing can actually reach the companies.

---

## Lane A — Edge / on-device AI infrastructure
**Creandum portfolio here: zero.**

*Why this lane.* A Creandum partner published, in May 2026: *"Local models become
genuinely useful at 64GB of RAM and above... As hardware and models improve, edge AI gets
more capable faster than most people realize. Dependence on frontier APIs will decrease,
not increase. AI will live in pockets, watches, glasses — in two years, not five."*
That is a stated, dated, public forecast with no corresponding bet. It is the cleanest
"stated appetite, unfilled" gap in the entire portfolio.

*Why now.* Three unlocks landed together: small models crossed the usefulness threshold;
NPUs shipped in volume across phones, PCs and industrial edge boxes; and EU data-residency
pressure made "the data never leaves the device" a procurement requirement rather than a
nice-to-have.

*What to look for.* Runtimes and compilers that make one model run across heterogeneous
NPUs; on-device fine-tuning and personalisation; fleet management and OTA for edge models;
edge inference for machines that cannot phone home (vehicles, factories, medical devices,
defence).

*Risk.* Adjacent to a brutal open-source commodity layer (llama.cpp, ONNX Runtime, Core ML).
The bar is a business model that survives free alternatives.

---

## Lane B — The European sovereignty stack: dual-use, security, critical infrastructure
**Creandum portfolio here: zero cybersecurity, zero defence.**

*Why this lane.* Creandum has already written the argument themselves. The PAVE Space memo
(Mar 2026) justifies a non-AI hardware bet on the grounds that *"Europe lacks an independent
[capability]... a strategic gap that needs ownership,"* citing €55B of European defence
spending. They nominated Arondite (defence autonomy) and Tracebit (security canaries) to
their own Euro Seed 50 and backed neither. The thesis is pre-argued and the slot is empty.

*Why now.* European defence budgets are in a structural step-change; NIS2 and DORA moved
security and operational resilience from IT budget to board liability; and sovereign-cloud
procurement rules now actively advantage European vendors over US incumbents for the first
time in a decade.

*What to look for.* Dual-use autonomy and sensing; security for OT/industrial and space
assets; compliance-as-infrastructure for NIS2/DORA; European alternatives where US vendor
lock-in has become a stated procurement risk.

*Risk.* Procurement cycles can be glacial and revenue lumpy. Needs evidence of a commercial
(not grant-funded) motion.

---

## Lane C — Vertical AI agents in sectors Creandum has *not* already bought
**Portfolio here: dense, but with specific holes.**

*Why this lane.* It is where Creandum actually deploys most capital — 80% of 2025–26 deals
are AI, and the Euro Seed 50 is >90% AI-native. Excluding it entirely would be picking a
fight with their revealed preference. But it is consensus by construction, so it only earns
a place if the sector is one they have *not* covered.

*Already taken (do not pitch):* legal (Vesence), recruiting (Jack & Jill), ERP migration
(Conduct), construction (Foresight), manufacturing QC (Cerrion), customer support/voice
(GetVocal), marketing (Superscale), enterprise automation (Maisa, Instruct, Ares),
accounting (Rillet, Sibill), treasury (Embat), supply-chain risk (Prewave).

*Holes worth sourcing:* healthcare operations beyond scribing (Junction is data
infrastructure, not workflow); energy and grid operations; public-sector and municipal
back-office; veterinary, dental and allied health; insurance claims adjudication.

*Risk.* Crowded. Bar for differentiation is a proprietary data or distribution position,
not a better prompt.

---

## Why three lanes
Lane A and Lane B are the "before it's obvious" bets — they carry the real upside for this
exercise because a partner has publicly wanted them and nobody has brought one. Lane C is
the control: it keeps the process honest and gives the memo a credible answer to *"what
else did you look at, and why did you reject it?"*

Weighting going into Phase 2 sourcing: **A 35% / B 35% / C 30%.**
