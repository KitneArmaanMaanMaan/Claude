# The heresy screen

The first pass scored companies on quality. This one scores them on **how wrong the market
thinks they are** — because a company everyone agrees with cannot produce a non-consensus
return, however well run it is.

## The question

> What does this company believe that almost everyone qualified to have an opinion believes
> is wrong? And what would have to be true for the crowd to be the ones who are mistaken?

A heresy is not an unfashionable sector. Grid software is unfashionable; "AI needs clean
data" is orthodox. A heresy is a claim that, if true, invalidates something the market has
already spent billions assuming.

## Scoring (100 points)

| Dimension | Wt | What earns points |
|---|---|---|
| **Heresy depth** | 30 | How central is the belief being denied? Denying a tactic scores low; denying where the entire industry is putting its capital scores high |
| **Founder conviction** | 25 | Evidence of having held the position *before* it was fundable, and of refusing to hedge. Control retained, not sold |
| **Falsifiability** | 15 | Is there a dated, public claim that could be shown wrong within 18 months? Heresy without a testable prediction is just contrarianism |
| **Asymmetry** | 15 | If right, is it a category rather than a company? The payoff has to justify the probability |
| **Creandum can act** | 15 | Europe, at most seed, ownership available, no conflict, and a fund that has shown appetite for frontier bets |

Deliberately **not** scored: traction, revenue, near-term market size. Those are the
dimensions that make consensus companies win, and applying them here reproduces the original
error.

### Eliminations and why

- **AMI Labs (76)** — the purest anti-LLM heresy in Europe, and Yann LeCun has held the
  position publicly for years against enormous pressure. Scores **0 on "Creandum can act"**:
  roughly $1B raised at a $3B pre-money. There is no seed to lead and no ownership to take.
- **Biomemory (66)** — genuinely heretical (the market believes DNA storage is decades from
  economic viability) and the conviction is real. Eliminated on stage: $18M Series A, and it
  has since acquired Catalog Technologies. Past the gate.
- **Stanhope AI (75)** — Karl Friston commercialising the free energy principle is about as
  heretical as European AI gets, and he has pushed it for twenty years while the field
  ignored him. Marked down on falsifiability (active inference has no dated public test that
  resolves in 18 months) and on actionability: an $8M seed led by Frontline closed in
  February 2026, so the round is gone and the next one is a year out.
- **Neuromorphic computing** — screened out as a *category*. Unconventional AI raised a
  $475M seed at $4.5B post led by a16z and Lightspeed with Sequoia, Lux and Bezos
  participating. Whatever this was in 2024, in 2026 it is consensus.
- **Formal verification** — screened out for the same reason. Axiom raised $200M; Pramaana
  Labs launched with $27M from Khosla. The market has arrived.
- **Squid (51)** — scores 15/15 on actionability and 4/30 on heresy. That asymmetry is the
  whole finding. It is a good company whose central claim — that AI needs a clean, governed
  data layer — is something every investor already nods along to. It stays in the pack as
  the documented low-variance alternative, not as the recommendation.

## The winner

**Frontier Computing, 96/100.** The only candidate that scores full marks on heresy depth,
falsifiability and asymmetry simultaneously. It loses 3 points on actionability because
General Catalyst got there first — but the round was a pre-seed on SAFEs, the founder still
holds 75%+ of shares and votes, and no priced round exists. Creandum can still lead the seed.
