# Deep dive 2 — Seeing Systems, London (runner-up)

## What it is
Inexpensive, autonomous strike drones for contested environments. Hardware modularity plus
an agentic control system that lowers operator cognitive load, claiming >2x reduction in
lifecycle cost. Their own description: *"Anduril, but with worse weather, and better banter."*

## Stage and eligibility
YC Winter 2026, London, team of 7, listed as Early stage. No priced round found. Clears
gates 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7.

## Why it nearly won
**The traction is the best on the entire longlist.** Customers and partners include the
**UK Royal Marine Commandos, several other UK units, and four other NATO forces**, with
prototypes already shipping for iterative feedback. Seven people with five national
militaries engaged is extraordinary, and it is exactly the kind of real-world deployment
signal Creandum praised in the Sereact memo.

The macro is equally strong. European defence, security and resilience startups raised a
record $8.7B in 2025, up 55% year on year — 43% of all European deeptech funding. Creandum
themselves built the PAVE Space investment case on €55B of European defence spending and a
"strategic gap that needs ownership." The thesis is pre-argued in their own words.

## Why it did not win
1. **Creandum may be structurally unable to do it.** Zero of 154 portfolio companies are in
   defence, and none have ever been in weapons. Many European LP agreements carry explicit
   exclusions on lethal and autonomous weapons systems. A recommendation the fund cannot
   legally execute is not a recommendation. This is a mandate question, not a taste question,
   and it costs the company half its Creandum-fit score.
2. **It is no longer non-consensus.** "European Anduril" is among the most crowded trades in
   European venture right now — E2D just launched a €500M defence and dual-use vehicle, the
   NATO Innovation Fund is active, and Ground A raised €9M+ pre-seed six months after
   founding. Creandum's brief is to back things *before it's obvious*. This is obvious.
3. **Hardware economics.** Mass-producible strike drones need manufacturing capital that a
   generalist €500M fund is poorly shaped to lead alone.
4. Sovereign customers mean lumpy, politically-exposed revenue and long procurement.

## If the mandate allows it
If Creandum's LPA permits defence, Seeing Systems is a defensible seed lead and the pull
evidence is stronger than Squid's. The recommendation would flip on that one legal fact —
which is worth establishing internally before the next defence company comes through the door,
because on current evidence the fund is leaving a whole category on the table by default
rather than by decision.
