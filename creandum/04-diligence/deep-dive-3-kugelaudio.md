# Deep dive 3 — KugelAudio, Berlin

## What it is
An enterprise-ready text-to-speech model that runs **on-prem or in the customer's own
cluster**, covering 30+ languages and dialects natively — Bavarian German, Northern Irish
English, Yemeni Arabic — and handling the cases that break generic TTS: email addresses,
phone numbers, product names. Low latency by design.

## Stage and eligibility
YC Spring 2026 (P26), Berlin, team of 4. Roughly $500k raised per third-party data, no
primary confirmation, no priced round found. Not on the Euro Seed 50. Clears all gates.

## Why it scored well
- **Lane A, precisely.** A Creandum partner wrote in May 2026 that *"dependence on frontier
  APIs will decrease, not increase"* and that edge AI is arriving "in two years, not five."
  KugelAudio is that forecast in product form, and the portfolio contains nothing like it.
- **Real distribution evidence.** They open-sourced their first model and passed **150k+
  downloads on Hugging Face** before YC. That is the open-source-to-revenue pattern the
  Euro Seed 50 analysis singled out, and it is pull that does not depend on a sales team.
- **Founder–market fit.** Kajo Kratzenstein spent two years on TTS research at
  Hasso-Plattner-Institut and built the model; he also shipped a production aneurysm-detection
  model at Floy. Viktor Presber scaled a recruiting firm to €300k ARR. They have founded
  together before (Sagemode) — the repeat-team signal Creandum paid up for at Hosted·ai.
- **Sovereignty tailwind.** On-prem voice is a procurement requirement in European public
  sector, health and finance, not a preference.

## Why it did not win
1. **Adjacency to GetVocal.** Creandum backed GetVocal — "the AI platform for human-AI
   workforces", a horizontal voice AI play — in November 2025. KugelAudio is a model and
   infrastructure layer rather than an application, and could plausibly be a supplier rather
   than a competitor. But the distinction takes a paragraph to explain, and a portfolio
   collision you have to argue your way out of is a weaker pitch than one that does not exist.
2. **Model-layer margin risk.** TTS quality is improving fast and cheaply across the open
   ecosystem. The defensible business may be the deployment, compliance and language coverage
   around the model rather than the model itself — which is a smaller company.
3. **TAM.** Enterprise on-prem TTS is a real market but a narrower one than grid infrastructure
   or defence; the €1B case needs them to become the broader European on-prem speech stack.

## What would change it
Evidence of paid on-prem deployments in regulated European enterprises at six-figure ACVs,
plus a credible path from TTS into ASR and full duplex voice. If they own the whole on-prem
speech stack rather than one model in it, this becomes a much bigger company.
