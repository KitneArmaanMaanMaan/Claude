### The structural gap nobody will say out loud

**This firm buys and operates companies. Your franchise is buying funds and LP stakes.**

Your centre of gravity is LP / fund-of-funds / secondaries — commitments, re-ups, SMAs, GP stakes, secondary positions. This role is control buyout of founder-led UK SMEs, with real portfolio-operations content: bolt-ons, business planning, sitting with founders on value creation. Your direct and co-investment reps are genuine ($20M ODM/OEM, $25M CX SaaS, $16M Series B) but they are the minority of your book, and several were executed in an advisory capacity supporting a client's deal team rather than as the principal.

**Gaps identified:**
1. **Modelling currency.** You will face a timed LBO test. You have not been the person in the model since roughly your Associate years.
2. **Principal vs. advisory.** TresVista is the outsourced partner. You directed deal teams and were embedded across a sovereign platform, but you have never held the pen on a fund's own capital. A PE screening partner will notice.
3. **Buyout portfolio operations.** Fund monitoring ≠ owning a founder-led business's operating plan.
4. **No accounting qualification.** The JD explicitly asks for accounting skills. The modal UK PE associate is ACA-qualified out of Big 4 TS, or a bulge-bracket IB analyst. You are CFA Level I with a B.Com.
5. **UK work authorisation.** Indian national in Mumbai; Skilled Worker sponsorship required.

**Mitigations (framing, not fabrication):**
- On modelling: do not claim currency you lack. Say you will sit a modelling test cold and prepare for it — then actually rebuild an LBO from blank before any interview.
- On principal vs. advisory: reframe honestly as *"I have run the analytical process behind $2.5B+ of capital deployment for the investors making the decision, and directed $6.5B+ across 26 transactions in 15 months on a sovereign platform. What I have not done is sign the cheque. That is exactly the seat I want next."* This is credible and disarms the objection before they raise it.
- On sponsorship: raise it in the first call. Do not let it surface at offer stage.

## C. Level & Positioning Strategy

**Target level:** Associate — 3–5 years, individual contributor, model-and-papers execution seat.
**Your level:** Product Head with P&L across a 250-person unit and 75+ mandates; VP since 2021; fast-tracked Analyst→VP in 5 years.
**Verdict: you are overqualified on tenure and scope, and under-credentialled on principal-investing reps.** That is an unusual and awkward combination, and it is the crux of this application.

### The question you cannot answer well

> *"You run a P&L and a 250-person business. Why do you want an Associate seat?"*

Every honest answer creates a new problem:
- *"I want to get back to investing"* → "Then why did you spend two years building AI products and rewrite your CV around it?"
- *"I want the principal seat I never had"* → credible, but invites "so you will leave the moment a VP seat opens elsewhere."
- *"Comp and London"* → reads as opportunistic.

There is no version of this conversation where you are not explaining a step down. A firm hiring a 3–5 year Associate wants someone hungry, mouldable, and staying three years. You will read as a flight risk who may be difficult to manage — and you would likely report to a VP with less tenure than you.

**If you proceed anyway**, the only positioning with a chance:

Do not apply as an Associate. **Contact the Pearse recruiter directly and ask to be considered at Senior Associate or VP level**, or for a different mandate on their desk. Pearse runs a dedicated secondaries practice — that is where your ten years actually price. Frame it as: *"I saw the Associate mandate. My background is heavier than that band, but I am genuinely interested in moving from the advisory side to a principal seat. What do you have at Senior Associate or VP, ideally secondaries or LP-focused?"*

That single email is worth more than this application.
